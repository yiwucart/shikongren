> **Translator's note.** The shortest case in the volume, and the one that states the method's boundary condition: only a movement or environmental change that happens *while you are watching*, or one that first catches your attention, carries predictive force. The rest of the method is a table of correspondences — a bee is money and feeling — applied to the geometry of the event.

---


A student asked: a bee circled the person being observed and then flew off in the X direction. How does the Shikongren method read that?

Teacher Lü's analysis:

1. A bee stands for money and for feeling.

2. A bee circling him means entanglement in feeling or in money and property.

3. The bee flying off in the X direction means that recently, and at the point marked X, he will suffer a loss in feeling and a drain of money.

The student's feedback on the spot: in the year X he divorced, lost money, was hurt emotionally, and the paperwork of the divorce was troublesome in every way.

Teacher Lü's caution:

The movement or environmental change must occur **while you are observing the person** for it to have predictive meaning. Or, if you were not observing him, his movement must first have caught your attention — and from the moment it caught your attention and you began to observe him, that movement carries predictive meaning too.
