> **Translator's note.** The most useful piece in this layer for anyone who has ever bought a Wen Chang pagoda the night before an exam. Two things carry it. The first is a distinction the system takes seriously — the output star produces *cleverness*, the officer star produces *judgement*, and they are not the same gift. The second is an honest statement of the remedy's time constant: five years can be promised, one month cannot, and the pagoda sold at the door of the examination hall can only add a few marks.

Study is adjusted through two things, and the order matters: the **seal star**, which governs examination results, and the **output star**, which governs the ability to learn. Results first, capacity second.

But before the method, a distinction that the author spends several pages establishing, because it decides what the adjustment can be aimed at.

## Cleverness is not judgement

Take a homely illustration. A woman can be clever enough to catch her husband in an affair — clever enough to arrange it, follow him, collect the evidence. That takes real ability. And then the question: to what end? To prove it? To divide the property? To stay married afterwards? To weigh what the children and the grandparents will feel? To consider, honestly, what happens if he decides she has become a problem?

Catching him is cleverness. Knowing what to do next is judgement.

In the ten-god scheme, the output star is the first and the officer star is the second. **The output star governs reading, examinations, technical mastery — the ability to become a good engineer, a good technician, a good research scientist.** All of that is cleverness. The officer star governs the capacity to face a crisis and resolve it by the best available route. It is judgement.

The two are not allies. In this reading they are close to opposites: the more purely clever a person is, the less judgement they tend to have, and the wiser a person is, the more their wisdom contains cleverness as a subordinate part. Cleverness is a partial and low-order expression of judgement; judgement is what includes it.

There is a consequence the author draws that is worth stating plainly, because it is sociological rather than technical. A person with the output star in close relation to the day master is clever and tends toward the self. A person with the officer star in close relation to the day master will show responsibility at the decisive moment — and may be found anywhere in the social scale, including the bottom of it. Rank and income do not follow from this. Conduct does.

The author's example is deliberately unglamorous: soldiers who were never officers, who in a moment of crisis did what the situation required while their cleverer comrades did not. Whether or not one accepts the metaphysics, the observation is not empty — the correlation between examination success and conduct under pressure is loose enough to be worth explaining, and no one has explained it well.

(Much of the surrounding material — about eggs, milk, and what modern diets have done to attention — is the author's aside on why a civilisation can grow more clever while losing its grip on wholes. It is kept here in one sentence because it is a mood, not an argument.)

## The method

Having separated the two, the ordinary procedure is straightforward: **make the seal star favourable first, then the output star.**

The non-ordinary procedure is where the work is. Find:

- Which stems and branches allow the seal star to reach the day master?
- Which allow the output star to reach it?
- Which allow both to reach it at once?

Adjust those specific characters, in the environment, and the child's study moves in the direction it should.

### Why the chart alone cannot answer

Before the case, the argument that holds the whole system together — and the reason the adjustment can claim more than a reading can.

Take a child with this exact chart. Will he pass the postgraduate examination next year? Read the chart as a pure time system and you can say yes or no. **And you will be right about half the time.**

Then read one more character. Right about half of that half. Then another. Half of half of half.

This is why, on the author's account, chart-only divination cannot be accurate — not because the reading is poor but because the chart is one input among several, and treating it as the only input costs you fifty per cent on each additional factor. What separates two children born in the same hour of the same day is not the chart. **It is where they are.** Which direction their home lies from the ancestral grave; which direction their school lies from their home; which direction the bedroom faces. The chart says what the energy is. The environment says where it is coming from.

## The worked case

> **Male: 己卯 丁卯 甲申 甲子** (preparing for the postgraduate examination)
> Luck pillar: 乙丑
> Year: 庚子

He wants to sit the postgraduate entrance examination. The adjustment can strengthen the energy available and can make him work; it cannot add knowledge he has not acquired.

**The two stars.** 子 water is the seal star (印, *yin*) and is favourable. 丁 fire is the output star (食伤, *shishang*) and is favourable.

**Does 子 reach the day master 甲?** Yes, by two routes. 子 is the hour branch, and the hour stem is also 甲 — the seal star sits directly beneath the day master. And in the 庚子 year, 子 combines with the day branch 申, bringing it under the day master again. Seal star favourable, and in close relation: good.

**The feng shui adjustment.** The 子 direction of both the ancestral grave and the current dwelling must be brought into strong favourable condition.

Here is the test of the whole argument. Every child with this chart is not equally likely to pass. Some will, some will not, and the chart cannot tell you which. What tells you is the 子 direction. Favourable sand there, and the examination goes well; hostile water there, and it fails. Same chart, opposite outcome, one variable.

**Does 丁 reach the day master?** Yes. 丁 fire, favourable.

**The 丁 adjustment.** Same logic, sharper illustration. Every child with this chart is not equally intelligent. Some are excellent; some are barely able to hold a pair of chopsticks, and no amount of teaching changes which hand they use. What separates them is whether the 丁 direction holds strong favourable water or strong hostile sand.

Which yields a claim that is easy to test and hard to believe: **a child educated in the 丁 direction is bright, with this chart, almost regardless of anything else.** And a child educated in the 子 direction does well in examinations. Twenty-four possible directions for a school, twenty-four different educational outcomes from one chart.

**Direction of travel.** Here the two answers diverge, and this is the subtlety worth taking away.

Feng shui says: make the 子 direction favourable. Travel says: **go to 丁, not to 子.** Because in the coming year wood is unfavourable, and travelling north to sit the examination invites a failure that will look inexplicable afterwards.

There is a further distinction, about credentials rather than marks. Go to 午 and the result may be the best paper in the country and no place. The author's example: second highest score nationally, one place at the institution, and the place goes to the first. 午 does not deliver the seal of a higher degree — the document cannot arrive from that direction. 丁 can.

**Who blocks the connection?** 庚. The year stem 庚 clashes with the day master 甲 directly, and a clashing stem does not want 甲 near its seal star or its output star. 庚 has to be handled, or the other two adjustments will be undone at the last moment: the paper is written well, the application is redirected, the place is lost to a调剂 — a reassignment to a different institution.

So the full set is **子, 丁 and 庚**, all three brought into favourable condition. Two out of three is not two-thirds of the result. It is a failure with a good excuse.

### The time constant

This is the passage to keep.

Everything above describes a standing adjustment — a change to the environment that is made and then left in place. Given a year, or even six months, the author will say that the examination will be passed. **Given a month, or a week, no one can promise that** — not because the method stops working, but because a change in the configuration needs time to become a change in the person. The most that can be promised at short notice is that the candidate performs above their level on the day.

Which is not the same as passing. A candidate who scores zero and is adjusted the night before scores ten. A candidate near the cut-off who is adjusted the night before scores ten above the cut-off. **The pagoda sold outside the examination hall can add marks. It cannot add years of study.**

The author is blunt about why a consultant should say this out loud, and the reason is commercial rather than ethical: the customer who expects a guarantee and does not get one leaves; the customer who is told the truth, sees the modest improvement, and stays for ten years is worth more. Telling the truth about the size of the effect is the better business, and it happens also to be true.

> A child who scores zero at the start of senior secondary school can be brought to a university place in three years, if the adjustment begins then. The same child adjusted in June of the final year goes from zero to ten.

### The rest of the prescription

**Food.** Avoid 丑, 午, 卯 and 子; favour 辰.

- **Beef (丑):** 丑 combines with 子 and draws down the seal star's energy. The examination is scored on the seal star, so beef works against the result.
- **Horse, donkey, mule (午):** 午 clashes 子 away. Same mechanism, more direct.
- **Hare (卯):** the day master is 甲 wood, and wood is unfavourable this year; 卯 is the peer star (比劫) and stirs it. The practical effect is restlessness — the inability to sit still and read. Half an hour less reading a day is 183 hours a year, and 183 hours is a body of knowledge. 卯 also punishes both 子 in the chart, spending the seal star twice over.
- **Fish (子):** water feeds the unfavourable wood. Not this year.
- **Shellfish (辰):** favourable. 辰 combines with 子 and with 申, bringing both under the day master; and 辰 harms both 卯, draining the unfavourable wood.

**People.** Keep distance from those born in 丑 or 午 years, and from those born in 甲, 卯, 乙 or 庚 years. A roommate from one of those years is not a small matter: 庚 clashes 甲, which removes the day master from the whole enterprise — a student with a 庚-year partner will talk about the examination and not sit it.

**Release.** Release hares, if releasing is your practice. 卯 is unfavourable, so releasing it is favourable. Do not release oxen: 丑 carries the seal star away.
