It states the ceiling: an adjustment moves a person *within* the band their chart allows, never across it. A clerk can become a section chief; a section chief cannot become a minister, and the author says why he would not be writing this if they could. It is the strongest self-limitation in the archive, and the best evidence that the archive knows where its claims stop.

Before the method, the limit.

An adjustment has a band. Inside the band it works reliably; at the edge of the band nothing happens at all, and the consultant who implies otherwise is lying to a customer who will eventually find out.

The distinction is between **improvement within a level** and **movement between levels.** Only the first is claimed.

## What a level is

The chart sets a frame. Not a script — the frame does not decide whether you will be happy, or when you will marry, or what you will do on a Tuesday. It sets the order of magnitude available: the rough height of the ceiling.

Call it a band. A man whose band runs from clerk to county-level official may spend his whole career as a section chief doing a county official's work — carrying the responsibility, not holding the post. That is not the band being denied. It is the band being occupied badly.

## What the remedy can do inside the band

This is the list of real effects, and it is more substantial than it sounds to a reader expecting miraculous claims:

- **From a nominal post to a real one.** The title without the authority becomes the title with it.
- **From a small jurisdiction to a large one.** The same rank, in a bigger county.
- **From a poor region to a prosperous one.** Same grade, different economy, different life.
- **From a marginal department to a central one.** Same seniority, different distance from the decisions.
- **From deputy to principal.** Within the band, the last step is often the one the remedy is asked for.

None of these are small. A man who moves from a nominal post in a poor peripheral county to a real post in a prosperous central one has changed his income, his children's schooling, his marriage prospects and his standing for the rest of his life — and every one of those moves happens inside the same band. An adjustment that reliably delivers them would be worth a great deal.

## What it cannot do

Cross the band.

The author's illustration is deliberately blunt. If the band runs to county level, then no adjustment produces a provincial or ministerial post. Not a difficult case, not an expensive case, not a case that requires more sessions. Not possible.

> If it were possible, I would not be spending my life teaching it. **I would have adjusted myself first.**

That is the argument, and it is a good one, because it does not depend on any theory. It depends on the observable behaviour of people who claim unlimited power: they are not, themselves, in possession of what they sell. The person selling a method that crosses levels is the first person who should have used it, and has not.

There is a second, quieter consequence. A consultant who promises the crossing is not merely overselling — he is guaranteeing a failure that the client will attribute to the consultant's competence rather than to the frame. Which is why the author's instruction to practitioners is unusually direct for a commercial document: **do not let the customer believe you can do this**, because the disappointment, when it arrives, will be worse than the sale was good.

## Why the limit is stated so loudly

Three reasons, and the third is the one that matters.

**Because it is true.** The frame is the frame; a method that cannot move it should not pretend to.

**Because a truthful promise is a better sale.** A customer told he can be moved to the top of his band, and moved there, becomes a customer for twenty years. A customer told he can be moved to the top of the country, and moved nowhere, becomes an enemy with a story.

**Because the limit is what makes the claim testable.** A promise with no ceiling cannot be checked — every outcome is compatible with it. A promise with a ceiling can be: put the adjustment in front of a band edge and see whether the person crosses. They will not. And the same test can then be applied inside the band, where the claims are, and where they can be checked one at a time.

The distinction is not modesty. It is the difference between a claim that could be wrong and a claim that has been arranged so that it cannot be.
