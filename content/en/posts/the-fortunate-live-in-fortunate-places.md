> **Translator's note.** 福人居福地 — “the fortunate live in fortunate places” — is the most quoted line in the whole tradition, and also the most useful to the person quoting it. The argument below takes it apart with three questions and no technical vocabulary. It is the shortest piece in this batch and possibly the most damaging, because it requires nothing from the reader except consistency.

---


There is a saying every practitioner knows: the fortunate live in fortunate places. It sounds like wisdom. It is a way of never being wrong.

The demolition takes three questions.

## First question

If you are a person of fortune, and a person of fortune will naturally end up on a good site, then what do you need a feng shui master for?

Either the site finds you or it does not. If it finds you, the consultation is unnecessary. If it does not, then fortune is not sufficient to produce a good site, and the saying is false at the first clause.

## Second question

If the fortunate can live in fortunate places by virtue of being fortunate, then what is the master for at all? I have the merit; every place I choose is a good place; I do not need to pay anyone.

Practitioners still charge. That is the tell. A doctrine that makes the practitioner unnecessary would not be repeated by practitioners for a thousand years unless it were doing some other work.

## Third question

If the fortunate live in fortunate places, then feng shui theory does nothing. The site is not what made the household fortunate; the household's fortune is what made the site. The causal arrow points the other way, and the entire technical apparatus — the forms, the directions, the sand and water, the compass — is idle machinery.

## What the saying is for

So why does everyone repeat it?

Because it converts every outcome into a verdict on the client.

- If you prosper, the site was good. The master chose well. He collects.
- If you suffer, you lacked fortune. The site may have been good; you were not the person it was good for. He collects nothing, and owes nothing.

Notice that no observation can damage this position. Prosperity confirms the master. Disaster confirms the client. There is no third possibility in which the reading was simply wrong, and therefore no possibility of a refund, an apology, or a corrected method.

## The comparison that matters

A doctor who tells you that the treatment works if your body cooperates is saying something true and specific: there are measurable limits, and here they are. A practitioner who says the same thing without ever naming a limit is saying something else — that he has no idea whether his method works, and has arranged the language so that he will never have to find out.

The difference is not cynicism. It is whether the claim can be checked. A claim that assigns all success to the technique and all failure to the client's character is not a claim about the world. It is a claim about the client, made after the fact, in whichever direction protects the fee.
