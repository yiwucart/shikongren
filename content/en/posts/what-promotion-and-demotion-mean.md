> **Translator's note.** A definition, and a useful corrective. In a Chinese reading, “promotion” does not mean promotion. It means anything the ordinary person would recognise as an improvement in work and career — including a driver getting a newer vehicle, or a teacher moving from a non-core subject to a core one. This is why clients and readers talk past each other: the client hears “you will be promoted” and looks for a title, when the chart may be flagging a shift of desk.

---


**In fate calculation, “promotion” means advancement in post and development in career.**

It covers, specifically: direct elevation in rank; a step up in professional title; the growth of a business; a student's progress; success in an examination; winning a prize; taking up a post; entering employment; an expansion of authority; a transfer with no change of rank from a small or poor unit to a large or good one, or from a small place to a large one; a move from countryside to city; a change of position for someone whose whole career has been unremarkable; and ordinary progress at work —

a driver exchanging an old vehicle for a good one; a teacher moved from teaching into administration; a teacher moved from a lower year or a non-core subject to a higher year or a core subject; a teacher moved from being overlooked to being relied on.

**Whatever ordinary people would agree counts as “getting better” in work and career is the information of “promotion” in fate calculation.**

**In fate calculation, “demotion” means a fall in post and obstruction in career.**

It covers: being removed from office; failing to win a title; failing an examination; a decline in study; a transfer with no change of rank from a large or good unit or a large place to a small or poor one, or from a small place to a smaller one; resigning; being stood down; moving from being relied on to being overlooked; a move from city to countryside with no change of rank; an ordinary person moved from a good position to a poor one; criticism from a superior; and ordinary decline at work.

**Whatever ordinary people would agree counts as “getting worse” in work and career is the information of “demotion.”**

Read the two lists against each other and the design is clear. The pair is not about rank. It is about **direction** — up or down — in the one domain that Chinese readers have always used as the index of a life's standing. That is why the vocabulary is so much wider than the words suggest, and why a reading that says “promotion this year” can be right about a man who never held a title in his life.
