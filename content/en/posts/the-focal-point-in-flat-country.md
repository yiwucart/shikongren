> **Translator's note.** A short, technical piece with an unusually clean rule in it. In flat country the old books looked for "water dragons," because rivers were the only large energy-carrying features on a plain. Modern roads do the same job, and there are far more of them. The essay gives the geometric construction for locating the strongest focal point at a bend or crossing — the third vertex of an equilateral triangle — and then a table for deciding whether a given bend helps or harms. The caveat at the end is the honest part: these rules apply to focal points, not to vein-qi points, and a vein-qi point outranks an angle of water entirely.

---


On a plain, a focal point can be formed where a road or a river bends or crosses.

**1. How to find a focal point.**

Where is the focal point? There is a criterion. (See the figure.)

![](/img/0429f167ad3bce64.jpeg)

The focal point lies on the perpendicular bisector of the line joining points A and B. There are many focal points along that bisector, but what we want is the one of greatest energy, and that one can be calculated: it is at point C, the third vertex of the equilateral triangle ABC, where AB = AC = BC.

On a plain, a focal point formed by a bend or crossing of a river or a road carries a great deal of energy — much more than the surrounding sand and water: the houses beside it, the crossing water, and the rest. The focal point's energy is of a different order.

Flat-country focal points are auspicious or inauspicious. Practice shows that an auspicious focal point certainly produces wealth and rank; an inauspicious one certainly brings serious misfortune.

The old books on the plains always speak of finding the **water dragon**. In the past there were no roads to speak of, let alone railways, so on a plain the only large dragon was the river. In modern flat country, roads, motorways and railways also form focal points of enormous energy — and there are far more roads today than there are rivers.

Reading feng shui on a plain today, the first thing to look at is the focal point formed where a road or a river bends or crosses, because that focal point outranks the energy of angle water and of all the other sand and water.

**Note carefully: focal points do not always lie on the centreline of the bend.**

![](/img/e3b75f24a105625f.jpeg)

This is because landforms differ, and the place where the vein energy gathers therefore differs. It is a physical cause. In reality, most river bends are irregular.

The focal point at C carries the greatest energy. The points around C carry progressively less.

**2. How to judge an auspicious or inauspicious focal point formed by a water dragon or a road dragon.**

**The auspicious and inauspicious character of sand and water by direction.**

**Taking the eight palaces:**

1. In the four directions **east, north, southeast and southwest**, sand is auspicious and water is inauspicious.

2. In the four directions **south, west, northeast and northwest**, water is auspicious and sand is inauspicious.

**By the same logic, focal points formed by water dragons and road dragons are judged as follows:**

1. In the four directions **east, north, southeast and southwest**, a focal point formed by a road dragon is auspicious, and one formed by a water dragon is inauspicious.

2. In the four directions **south, west, northeast and northwest**, a focal point formed by a water dragon is auspicious, and one formed by a road dragon is inauspicious.

![](/img/9d215186be412f53.jpeg)

Water in the east is inauspicious, so a focal point formed by a river bend in the east is inauspicious. Water in the west is auspicious, so a focal point formed by a river bend in the west is auspicious.

![](/img/4dcdcb69ab57dd90.jpeg)

Sand in the east is auspicious, so a focal point formed by a road bend in the east is auspicious. Sand in the west is inauspicious, so a focal point formed by a road bend in the west is inauspicious.

A road bend in the east gives an auspicious focal point. But someone will ask: a road bend in the east also forms inauspicious angle water — so which is it? As long as it is a true dragon point, as long as it is a vein-qi point, its energy is the greatest, and beside a vein-qi point, angle water counts for nothing.

**All of the above applies to focal points. If the point in question is not a focal point but a sand-and-water point, the points on either bank of the river or road are judged differently.**

On a plain, a focal point formed by a water dragon or a road dragon is in practice equivalent to a vein-qi point — but we cannot call it one, because it does not sit in the qi-bearing layer.
