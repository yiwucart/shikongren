> **Translator's note.** A short, argumentative piece about a definition. Most treatments of the four pillars call them a time system — eight characters recording the hour of birth. This one refuses, and the refusal is the foundation of everything else in this archive. The argument is a piece of logic, not a doctrine: time has no element, the stems and branches do, therefore the stems and branches are not time. If that step holds, the rest of the method follows; if it fails, the rest of the method has nothing to stand on. It is the most direct statement of the system's premises in the archive, and the shortest route into it for a reader who wants to know what is actually being claimed.

Almost everyone who studies the four pillars begins from the same premise: the eight characters are a **timestamp.** They record the year, month, day and hour of a birth, and the whole art consists of reading that timestamp.

This archive rejects the premise. Not as a quibble about terminology — as the thing that decides whether the method works.

## The argument

The stems and branches carry the five elements. 甲 is wood, 丙 is fire, 壬 is water. That is not disputed by anyone in the tradition.

Time does not carry the five elements.

Time has no mass, no quality, no texture, no direction. It is not a substance and cannot be one; nobody who has tried to say what time is *made of* has produced an answer, because time is not made of anything. If the stems and branches are elements — things with qualities — then they cannot be time. They may be *used to mark* time, in the way a name is used to mark a person, but the name is not the person.

The classical reply is that 甲 乙 丙 丁 *represent* time. They do represent it. But representation is not identity, and the moment you treat a representation as the thing itself, you have accepted a category error and then built a system on top of it. **The ordinary reading treats a substance as a timestamp and then wonders why the reading is unreliable.**

## What the eight characters are

If they are not a clock, what are they?

Each stem and branch carries four properties at once:

1. **A time** — the year, month, day or hour it marks.
2. **A direction** — one of twenty-four, or a half of one.
3. **An environmental character** — sand or water, high or low, at that direction.
4. **A body location** — a part of the body.

A chart, therefore, is not a record of when someone was born. It is a description of **what is inside them**: eight energies, arriving from eight directions, held in a body.

This is the sentence on which the entire system turns. A person is alive, on this account, because those eight energies are moving inside them in their proper order. Health is the order; disaster is a disturbance. When one of the eight stops moving as it should and begins to move somewhere it should not, the person suffers — and which disaster, and how bad, depends on which energy has moved, to where, and how violently.

## Two illustrations

The author uses two, and both are worth keeping because they do the work that doctrine cannot.

### The glass

Take the body as a glass. The chart tells you what is already in it: not just water, but sugar, vinegar, apple, banana juice — eight ingredients in particular proportions.

Then a year arrives. The year's 庚 energy is peach juice. Pour peach juice into a glass that already holds vinegar and wait three hours, and the glass smells of alcohol. Nothing was added that was not already accounted for; the taste changed anyway, because two substances met.

Now read a year without this picture. Read it as a timestamp and you have a date and no ingredients, and you will be reduced to guessing. **This is why chart-only fortune-telling produces confident answers and poor ones.**

### The porridge

The second illustration answers the strongest objection to the whole tradition: if the chart is a timestamp, why do two people born in the same hour have different lives?

Because the timestamp is not the porridge.

On the eighth day of the twelfth month, every household in a town cooks the same dish at the same hour. Every pot is different. One family's has more red dates, another's more green beans; one is sweet, one is thin. Same time, different contents — and the contents are what you taste. A chart with more wood is a pot with more of one thing, not a clock reading.

The illustration goes further, and this is the part that matters. Take one pot — your mother's. Is the first spoonful the same as the last? No. Same pot, same hour, different bowl. **Which is why even the same chart, born into the same household, does not produce the same person.**

## What follows

Three consequences, and they are the reason this piece sits at the front of the remedy layer rather than the back.

**First, an environment adjustment must be made against a chart.** If a chart were only a timestamp, adjusting a house would be a separate operation from reading a person, and the two would meet only by convention. Because the chart is a description of which directions' energies are already inside the body, the house is not a separate subject: it is where the incoming energy is coming from. You cannot adjust a direction without knowing what it will meet when it arrives.

**Second, the same house is not the same house for two people.** Two families in identical flats, with identical furniture in identical corners, are not in identical conditions, because the flats are being read against different bodies. This is the answer to the observation that feng shui “works for some people and not others” — the house did not change; the occupant did.

**Third, a year is not an event but an addition.** The year does not *cause* an outcome from outside. It adds an ingredient to a mixture that is already reacting, and the outcome is the reaction. Which is why the same year is a good year for one person and a ruinous one for another, and why the calendar cannot be read on its own.
