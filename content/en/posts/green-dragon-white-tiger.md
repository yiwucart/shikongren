> **Translator's note.** Almost every English-language feng shui text gives the four animals as *left* green dragon, *right* white tiger, *front* vermilion bird, *back* dark warrior. This essay argues that the left-right reading is a convenience that only holds when a building faces south, and that the animals carry fixed compass directions derived from the twenty-eight lunar mansions. The classical quotations are given in translation, with the Chinese titles in brackets for readers who want to check them.

---


The standard formula in the feng shui literature is *left green dragon, right white tiger, front vermilion bird, back dark warrior*. It is repeated as though it were a definition. It is not a definition, it is a shorthand — and a shorthand that silently assumes a building faces south. Take the shorthand for the rule and the readings go wrong the moment a building faces anywhere else.

## Where the four animals come from

The four animals did not begin as descriptions of a house. They are groupings of the twenty-eight lunar mansions (二十八宿) as those stars appear on the celestial sphere.

Join the stars of the seven eastern mansions with a line and the figure resembles a dragon. East belongs to wood, and the colour of wood is green, so the seven eastern mansions are called the Green Dragon.

Join the seven western mansions and the figure resembles a tiger. West belongs to metal, and the colour of metal is white, so the seven western mansions are called the White Tiger.

Join the seven northern mansions and the figure resembles a turtle. North belongs to water, and the colour of water is black, so the seven northern mansions are called the Dark Warrior.

Join the seven southern mansions and the figure resembles a bird. South belongs to fire, and the colour of fire is red, so the seven southern mansions are called the Vermilion Bird.

The derivation decides the assignment. The Green Dragon can only be east. The White Tiger can only be west. The Dark Warrior can only be north. The Vermilion Bird can only be south.

*East green dragon, west white tiger, south vermilion bird, north dark warrior* is therefore the only reading consistent with the astronomy the animals were drawn from. Turning it into *left, right, front, back* breaks the link to that astronomy entirely.

## What the derivation fixes

Once the four animals are anchored to compass directions, they stop being a description of the observer and become a property of the site.

That matters because left and right are not properties of a site. They are properties of a person standing on it. Walk around the same grave and the left side becomes the right side. The eastern mansions do not move when you walk.

## The passage in Guo Pu that starts the confusion

The *Book of Burial* (葬书) attributed to Guo Pu contains the line: *bury with the left as green dragon, the right as white tiger, the front as vermilion bird, the back as dark warrior.*

That single sentence is the source of the entire modern formula. But Guo Pu was the founding figure of the tradition. He cannot have been ignorant of where the four animals came from, and he cannot plausibly have made an error of this kind — an error that a later text points out in considerable detail.

The later text is *Guan's Geographical Guide* (管氏地理指蒙), chapter twenty-seven, "Explaining Left and Right":

> To call left and right "dragon" and "tiger" is to speak of a house sitting north and facing south. But if a grave faces east instead, then the north is named the Green Dragon's wood and the south is named the White Tiger's metal. The labels become perverse, the names tangled and out of order.
>
> Consider: the five notes take *gong* as their root, and *shang*, *jiao*, *zhi* and *yu* are ranged beneath it by kind. The centre takes earth as its standard, and the positions of metal, wood, water and fire divide from there. Only by taking the front, back, left and right of one's own body can one speak of the four configurations and get them true. For wood is green in colour and its virtue is figured as the dragon; metal is white and its virtue is figured as the tiger; water is black and its virtue is the dark warrior; fire is red and its virtue is the vermilion bird. The directions of these four figures and the four configurations of the body do not correspond.

The last sentence is the argument in miniature. The four animals belong to four fixed directions. The four "configurations" belong to the body of whoever is standing there. They are two different coordinate systems, and the formula that identifies them is a category error.

## Why Guo Pu was not wrong

If Guo Pu is right, then Guan is wrong. If Guan is right, then Guo Pu is wrong. If Guo Pu is right about left and right, then the derivation of the four animals is wrong. If the derivation is right, then Guo Pu's sentence is at least imprecise.

None of the principals can have made a basic error, and the derivation is not in doubt. So the only reading consistent with the facts of Guo Pu's own period is this: in his time, houses and graves were laid out to be **sheltered from the wind and open to the sun** (背风向阳). In the northern hemisphere, sheltered-and-sunlit means **sitting north and facing south**.

In that one orientation, and only in that one, left is east, right is west, front is south and back is north. The shorthand is exactly correct. Guo Pu used it because in his world it was always true, in the same way that a modern writer might say "the sun rises on your left" when describing a walk he always takes facing north.

The shorthand is not the doctrine. It is a description of the doctrine under one standard orientation, and it inherited the authority of the man who wrote it long after the orientation stopped being standard.

## The same shorthand in "first, second and third branch"

This is not the only place the tradition mistook a convenience for a rule. The same thing happened with the *zhangfang, erfang, sanfang* (长房、二房、三房) theory — the claim that the first, second and third "branches" of a family are affected differently by a grave, by branch meaning the eldest son's line, the second son's line, and so on.

Read strictly, the terms cannot mean the children who are presently alive in that order, because the reasoning collapses immediately.

Suppose the man who is now the eldest was not the first pregnancy. A miscarriage, a stillbirth, an infant death — any of these, and the person now called *zhangfang* is not the first-born at all. Suppose his father had a child by another woman before him, or his mother had a child by another man. The person now called *zhangfang* may be the third or fourth child of that parentage.

And if the terms track whoever is presently alive, then the theory produces absurdities on its own terms: the eldest dies, the second son becomes *zhangfang*, the second dies, the third becomes *zhangfang*. A category that changes occupant when someone dies cannot be the thing a grave acts upon.

The only coherent reading is that *zhangfang, erfang, sanfang* mean **the first, second and third birth order** — the first child borne, the second, the third — not the first, second and third son currently living. Once that is understood, the theory has a definite referent, and it is the same kind of shorthand as left-and-right: a convenient label that points at a real distinction and then gets mistaken for the distinction itself.

## What to test in the field

The practical claim is checkable, and it should be checked on real sites rather than argued about in the abstract.

Take a set of graves and dwellings, read each one twice — once assigning the animals by east, west, south and north, once assigning them by left, right, front and back — and compare which reading matches what is actually observed at the site.

The claim of this school is that the compass reading is substantially more accurate, and that the gap widens in proportion to how far a site departs from facing south. A site that faces south cannot distinguish the two methods, because there the two methods are the same method. That is exactly why the error survived: for centuries, most of the sites anyone bothered to read were the ones where the shorthand worked.
